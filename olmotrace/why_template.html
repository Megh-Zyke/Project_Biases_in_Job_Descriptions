Why OLMo Writes These Personas

Almost every one of OLMo's persona habits appears in a single step: supervised fine-tuning (SFT). Some are copied from the AI models that wrote the SFT data. Others are leanings the base model already had, which SFT turns into defaults. Preference tuning only nudges a few.

5 · Limitations and possible extensions

The evidence supports the claims above as attribution: where each pattern comes from and at which stage it appears. These are the limits on that, and what would tighten it.

Limitations

  • The base model got a plain completion prompt; SFT, DPO and Instruct got the chat prompt. Part of the base-to-SFT jump could come from the format, though hardly all of 67% → 92% women.
  • SFT amplifies the gender and age skews, but we can't say which part of SFT does it: imitating particular subsets (the synthetic math problems are 70% she/her) or fine-tuning collapsing onto the most likely answer.
  • The name evidence is correlational. Four sources agree, but no experiment removes the names from the data.
  • Names, ages and gender are detected with text patterns. Pronoun shares in the data count all text, not only personas.
  • infini-gram counts exact matches, and its later-stage counts mix mid-training, SFT and DPO.
  • About 15% of base-model completions only echoed the prompt and were dropped. The base model's age-32 share rests on 410 personas, its surname pairing on 24, and later stages mention spouses too rarely (6–30 times) to test same-sex spouses.

Possible extensions

  • Prompt the teacher models. Run the persona prompt on GPT-4o and Gemma-2-27B to show their defaults directly. Needs an OpenAI API key; Gemma runs on one A6000.
  • Swap the names in the SFT data. Fine-tune a small OLMo 2 base with PersonaHub and GSM8K names randomised. A causal test of the copying route; takes days.
  • Fine-tune on parts of SFT. Train with and without the synthetic math subsets to find what amplifies the gender and age skews. Also days.
